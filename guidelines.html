Road Safety

Driving Guidelines

Official safe-driving guidelines every learner should know — general rules of the road, what to do when you are driving tired, and how to keep children safe in cars.

Guidelines source: Nepal Traffic Police. These are the official driving guidelines published for drivers in Nepal. We teach every one of them in our courses at Alam Driving Institute.

General Guidelines

The everyday rules that keep you — and everyone around you — safe on the road.

  • Always carry your driving license & Blue Book while driving a vehicle.
  • Don't drink while driving.
  • Don't drive under the influence of drugs.
  • Obey all traffic signals, lights and signs.
  • Use the indicator or hand signals when changing lanes.
  • Adhere to permitted speed limits.
  • Don't use your cell phone while driving. If you must, move to the left, halt and then make the necessary call.
  • Irrespective of right of way, stay alert and be considerate to pedestrians — especially senior citizens, the handicapped, ladies and children.
  • Do not overload your vehicle with luggage or passengers.
  • Do not use tinted glasses, lenses or visors, or anything that restricts vision, at night or in poor visibility conditions.
  • Do not drink and drive, as it adversely affects your judgment and abilities.
  • Wear seat belts.
  • Always drive using the correct gear.
  • Avoid sudden braking and harsh acceleration.
  • Never use the clutch as a footrest while driving.
  • Do not overload your vehicle or trailer. Never tow a greater weight than recommended by the manufacturer of your vehicle.
  • Ensure all children under 14 years of age wear a seat belt or sit in an approved child restraint.

Driving When Tired

Fatigue slows your reactions like alcohol does. Follow these rules on every long drive.

  • Make sure you are fit to drive. Do not undertake a long journey (longer than an hour) if you feel tired.
  • Avoid undertaking long journeys between midnight and early morning hours, when natural alertness is at a minimum.
  • Plan your journey to include sufficient breaks. A minimum break of at least 15 minutes after every two hours of driving is recommended.
  • If you feel sleepy, stop at a safe place. Do not stop on the hard shoulder of a motorway.
  • The most effective ways to counter sleepiness are to drink two cups of strong coffee or take a short nap (up to 15 minutes).
  • Fresh air, exercise or turning up the radio may help for a short time, but are not as effective.

Children in Cars

Extra care keeps your youngest passengers safe on every trip.

  • Children should not sit behind the rear seats in an estate car or hatchback unless a special child seat is fitted.
  • Child safety door locks, where fitted, should be used when children are in the car.
  • Children should be kept under control.
  • A rear-facing baby seat should never be fitted into a seat protected by an airbag.
